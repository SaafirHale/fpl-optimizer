# Backtest report

Generated by `scripts/make_report.py` from the JSON files in this folder.

## Method

- **Walk-forward, no lookahead.** To project gameweek *t*, the model sees that season's rows from gameweeks before *t* plus the previous season. The fixture list and prices at *t* are known before the deadline. `tests/test_model.py::test_no_lookahead` rewrites every row from *t* onward and checks that the projection for *t* does not change.
- **Validation and test are separate seasons.** Model hyperparameters (`scripts/tune.py`) and the transfer policy (`scripts/tune_policy.py`) were chosen on **2024/25** only. **2025/26** is the hold-out season, evaluated with those settings.
- **Ground truth** is the points each player actually scored, from the public per-gameweek dataset (vaastav/Fantasy-Premier-League). The scoring tables reproduce 100% of those totals from their components in 2024/25 and 2025/26 (`test_scoring_tables_reproduce_every_actual_score`).
- **Rows:** every player registered in a gameweek whose club played (most score 0). "Top pick's actual pts" is the mean real score of each projection's single highest-projected player per gameweek, before the captain's double.

## Why FPL's same-week xP is not a baseline

The dataset's xP column for gameweek *t* looks like a forecast but was recorded after *t*. An honest forecast for *t* loses a little accuracy on *t+1*; this column correlates with *t* far better than with *t+1*, and as well with *t-1* as with *t* (2024/25, Pearson r with points):

| Column | Last week | This week | Next week |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| This model | 0.627 | 0.578 | 0.519 |
| FPL xP, same week | 0.712 | 0.731 | 0.479 |
| Form | 0.728 | 0.497 | 0.460 |
| Points per match | 0.616 | 0.512 | 0.487 |

So the comparison above uses the previous gameweek's snapshot instead.

## Season replay: 2025/26 with the optimizer

The solver runs every gameweek with a 3-week horizon, carrying the squad, bank, selling prices and free transfers forward. GW1 is a free draft on GBP 100m. Each week is scored on real points with automatic substitutions and the vice-captain rule. No chips are played. "Form" is the same solver fed the naive form projection.

| Projections | Season points | Before hits | Hits taken | Transfers | Best week | Worst week |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| This model | 2,005 | 2,005 | 0 | 35 | 90 | 30 |
| Form | 1,894 | 1,954 | 15 | 52 | 85 | 17 |

Sensitivity: with no extra hit cost (the policy before tuning), the same replay scored 2,059 with the model (10 hits) and 1,621 with form (76 hits).

Noisy projections make the solver chase every swing, so much of the gap is hits; the "before hits" column shows the gap without them. This compares the projections inside the same decision engine. It is not a comparison with real managers: the public data has no 2025/26 average manager score.

## Hyperparameters (chosen on 2024/25)

Coordinate search over 30 settings moved validation RMSE from 1.948 to 1.926 (1.927 with the final settings below). Nearly all of the gain came from the minutes model reacting faster (recency decay 0.6, prior worth one match). Shrinkage of 450 vs 900 prior minutes differed by less than 0.001 RMSE, so Valuo's 900 is kept.

## Limitations

- Historical injury and suspension flags are not archived, so the backtest model has no team news. Live runs use FPL's `chance_of_playing_next_round`; the backtest understates that.
- Defensive contributions began in 2025/26, so GW1 of the hold-out season has no prior for them; the pooled rate is learned from GW2 on.
- Prices are held flat over the planning horizon, and the replay plays no chips.
- Most player-gameweeks score 0 or 1, which keeps MAE low for every method; RMSE, correlation and the replay separate them better.
